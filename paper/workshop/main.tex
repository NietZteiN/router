\title{Deletion Without Absence:\\What a Modular Unlearner Answers Instead}

\author{%
  Anonymous Submission
}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Modular ``exact unlearning'' makes deletion cheap: train one small expert per data source, keep a
selector that routes each query to an expert, and delete a source by dropping its expert. The
guarantee is real but narrow --- no surviving parameter was ever trained on the removed data --- and
it says nothing about the queries that used to reach the removed expert. Those queries still
arrive, and the selector must send them somewhere. We audit where, on a 200-expert LoRA pool over
TOFU with 20 of 200 sources deleted. Three findings. (i)~The standard forget metric cannot see
substitution: a ``method'' that deletes nothing and merely reroutes the deleted sources' queries to
one arbitrary survivor scores at or above genuine deletion in six of seven destination choices at
byte-identical utility, moving \fq{} by $0.53$ on a choice unrelated to forgetting. (ii)~The
substitution is substantive: orphans are answered with a specific surviving author's distinctive
facts at $0.24$--$0.30$ against a $0.17$ random-destination floor, and refusal essentially never
happens ($\leq 0.013$ of 1600 answers). (iii)~Every reassuring property of the pattern --- orphan
detectability, deletion locality, routing accuracy --- is measured on queries that name the person
being deleted, and collapses when the name is removed. A routed system's deletion claim is
incomplete without reporting what it substitutes.
\end{abstract}

% ============================================================================================
\section{The queries survive the module}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine unlearning asks a system to behave as though it had never seen some training data
\citep{bourtoule2021sisa}. Retraining a large language model is prohibitive, so a substantial line
of work makes deletion cheap \emph{architecturally}: partition the training data by source, train
an isolated module per source, compose the modules at inference, and delete a source by dropping
its module \citep{yu2022legonet,basuroychowdhury2025s3t,zhao2024ramole,grimes2026memadapt,%
schneider2026sea}. The guarantee is structural rather than statistical --- afterwards, no surviving
parameter was ever updated on the removed data --- and it is narrower than it sounds. A modular
system needs a \emph{selector} to decide which module answers a query. Deletion removes a module;
it does not remove the queries that were routed to it. Every one of those queries still arrives,
and the selector must send it somewhere. We call such a query an \emph{orphan}, and this paper asks
one question: \textbf{what does the system say to an orphan?}

On a per-source expert pool over TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu}, it answers confidently with someone
else's life. Asked whether a deleted author had won any awards, the system names an award --- one
belonging to a different, still-present author, with the name substituted and the rest of the
sentence intact. It does this on roughly a third of orphaned queries and refuses on approximately
none of them. The deletion was exact. The output is a fabricated biography attached to a real name,
and the person whose facts were borrowed did not consent to that either.

Worse, the field's standard instrument scores this as success. TOFU's \fq{} is a two-sample test of
\emph{distributional indistinguishability} between the post-deletion model's forget-set behaviour
and a reference model retrained without the data --- and a stranger's answers are, in distribution,
about as far from the deleted author's memorised answers as a retrained model's are. We exhibit an
``unlearning method'' that deletes nothing, only redirects, and scores at or above genuine
deletion. The appendices carry the rest of the campaign, including four pre-registered hypotheses
we refuted and eight measurement defects that produced plausible numbers before they were caught
(App.~\ref{app:constraints}).

\section{Setup: three deletions, three metrics}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{The pattern under audit.} A frozen base model $f_0$; modules $\{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_k\}$
with $\phi_i$ trained only on source $i$; a selector $S$ mapping a query $q$ to one module. The
served answer is $f_0$ composed with $\phi_{S(q)}$, and deleting source $i$ removes $\phi_i$ from
the pool and from $S$'s candidate set. This covers keyed adapter banks, sharded PEFT unlearning,
retrieval-routed LoRA pools, per-user deletable proxies and block-listed memory modules
(App.~\ref{app:related}). The selector is the part that has not been audited.

\paragraph{Three things ``deleted'' can mean.} \textbf{D1 (parametric):} no parameter serving the
query was trained on the deleted source --- what modular architectures guarantee, by construction.
\textbf{D2 (behavioural):} the system does not answer as though it still held the source --- what a
data subject takes ``deleted'' to mean. \textbf{D3 (indistinguishability):} an observer with query
access cannot tell a deletion occurred. D1 implies neither, and this paper is about both gaps.

\paragraph{Metrics.} TOFU supplies \fq{} and \util{}; neither is a statement about what the system
substitutes. \orr{} is the fraction of orphaned queries answered substantively rather than refused,
measured on generated text rather than on routing. \csar{} is the fraction of orphan answers
asserting at least one fact that is distinctive to the survivor the query was routed to, is
\emph{not} the deleted source's own fact, and is \emph{not} in the frozen base's answer to the same
question. \rdr{} is the fraction of \emph{retained} queries that change module as a side effect of
a deletion nobody asked for. \csar{} is deliberately not an $n$-gram metric: ``Alice writes magical
realism and was born in Lisbon'' --- Bob's genre and city, asserted about Alice --- has low ROUGE-L
against every gold answer in the corpus and is exactly the harm (App.~\ref{app:metrics}).

\paragraph{Pool, selectors, transforms.} Llama-2-7B-chat frozen, $k=200$ rank-32 LoRA experts
\citep{hu2022lora}, one per TOFU author; deletion is \texttt{forget10} (authors 180--199, 400
orphaned questions). Eight selectors in three families: lexical (\texttt{key\_exact},
\texttt{key\_tfidf}), feature-space (\texttt{centroid\_sbert} over MiniLM embeddings
\citep{reimers2019sbert}, \texttt{centroid\_lm}), and behavioural (\texttt{ppl},
\texttt{activation\_norm}, \texttt{attn\_norm}, \texttt{logit\_div}), the last of which \emph{run}
the candidate experts. Because a harm measured only on queries that name the deleted person is
worth as much as a defense measured that way, every result is reported across a transform axis --- \texttt{gold} (TOFU's question, which
names the author in ${\sim}90\%$ of rows) versus \texttt{name\_stripped} and three others --- with
the perturbation applied to \emph{both} classes so no detector can learn ``perturbed $\Rightarrow$
deleted'' (App.~\ref{app:setup}--\ref{app:transforms}).

\section{The forget metric is blind to substitution}
\label{sec:blind}

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{TOFU \fq{} across reroute destinations. Six of the seven arms that delete nothing score
  at or above genuine deletion, while \util{} is \emph{identical to $0.8009$ in all eight}.}
  \label{tab:e5}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Destination & s89 & s137 & s31 & s97 & \textbf{DELETE} & s33 & s79 & s88 \\
    \midrule
    expert affinity & .3044 & .3382 & .2840 & .2267 & --- & .2663 & .2193 & .3970 \\
    \fq{}           & \textbf{.8958} & .8002 & .6288 & .6288 & \textbf{.5140} & .5140 & .5140 & .3615 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

We define an ``unlearning method'' that deletes nothing: all 400 orphaned queries are rerouted to
one fixed surviving expert, while every expert --- including the 20 supposedly forgotten ones ---
stays in the pool and in memory. Seven destination choices and one genuine-deletion baseline
(experts dropped, orphans served by the frozen base) are scored at the same tier against the same
held-fixed reference, so all eight cells are the same statistical test.

Table~\ref{tab:e5}: six of seven arms that delete nothing score at or above genuine deletion, two
land exactly on it, and \fq{} spans $0.3615$--$0.8958$ --- a swing of $0.53$ --- on a choice that
holds fixed whether anything was forgotten, while \util{} does not move at all. The obvious
explanation, that rerouting to a similar author makes the answers look right, is \emph{refuted}
(Spearman $\rho = +0.109$, $p=0.82$, nominally opposite in sign to the hypothesis).

The arms score identical rows, so resolving the spread requires a \emph{paired} interval;
bootstrapping each arm's marginal re-adds the noise the arms hold in common and can never resolve
any spread (App.~\ref{app:constraints}). Resampling row indices once per iteration and applying
that draw to every arm gives a spread 95\% CI of $[0.2245,\,0.6975]$, $P(\text{spread} > 0.25) =
0.961$, and an at-or-above-deletion count of $6/7$ with 95\% CI $[2,\,7]$: even at the pessimistic
end, two arms that delete nothing match or beat real deletion. Two limits travel with these numbers
--- the per-destination \emph{ordering} does not reproduce across evaluation tiers, so no claim may
name a winning destination, and a single \fq{} cell is worth only about $\pm 0.35$
(App.~\ref{app:e5}).

\section{What the system says instead}
\label{sec:csar}

    \texttt{gold}           & \texttt{key\_tfidf}      & 0.3650 & \textbf{0.2950} & 0.933 & 0.000 \\
    \texttt{name\_stripped} & \texttt{centroid\_sbert} & \textbf{0.4400} & --- & 0.273 & $\leq$0.010 \\
    \texttt{indirect}       & \texttt{centroid\_sbert} & 0.3350 & --- & 0.383 & $\leq$0.013 \\

    \bottomrule

\paragraph{Refusal never happens.} At most $0.013$ across 1600 answers: $\orr{} \approx 1.00$ at
the level of what is \emph{said}, not merely where the query is routed. The information needed to
refuse is often present and then discarded --- \texttt{key\_exact} raises no-match on $100\%$ of
orphans, falls back to \texttt{candidates[0]}, and answers anyway.

\paragraph{It is not a naming glitch.} The strongest objection to \csar{} is that it counts the
router emitting the survivor's \emph{name} in place of the deleted author's. Splitting each
cross-source row by whether its matched facts are exhausted by name-forms (Table~\ref{tab:csar}),
two thirds to four fifths of \csar{} carries a real fact --- a title, a place, an award --- and it
survives dropping the identity-question slice, where a wrong expert answers with a name by
construction ($0.217/0.250$ substantive on the 300 non-identity queries). \textbf{We therefore
quote the substantive figure, $0.24$--$0.30$ against a $0.17$ floor}, not the raw
$0.33$--$0.37$.

\paragraph{Anonymising the query does not reduce the harm; it changes its character.} Strip the
name and the system leaks \emph{less} of the deleted author's own content ($0.913 \to 0.273$) while
attributing \emph{more} of a stranger's content to them ($0.3325 \to 0.4400$). Attribution does not
require the router to be \emph{right}, only confident enough to pick someone: whichever expert is
activated asserts its own facts, and worse routing produces \emph{more legible} attribution.

\paragraph{The floor matters.} A uniformly random surviving expert --- no router at all --- already
produces $\csar{} = 0.2200$ ($0.1725$ substantive). The harm therefore \textbf{cannot be engineered
to zero by improving the selector}; roughly two thirds of it is a property of substituting any
expert at all. The quantity to report is the lift over that floor, not \csar{} itself.

\section{Every reassuring property is a lexical artifact}
\label{sec:lexical}

\paragraph{Granularity was the axis, and it does not survive anonymisation.} The published finding
that confidence-based orphan refusal caps at AUC $0.57$--$0.61$ turns out to be a statement about
\emph{coarse units}: at constant deletion size, detection rises monotonically with $k$
(\texttt{centroid\_sbert} $0.564 \to 0.795 \to 0.984$ for $k=10,50,200$). Strip the name and the
ladder flattens to $0.560 \to 0.572 \to 0.623$ --- $+0.063$ across a $20\times$ granularity change,
against $+0.367$ on gold-form queries (App.~\ref{app:detect}).

\paragraph{So does everything else that made the pattern look safe} (Table~\ref{tab:lexical}
collects the cells). Routing accuracy collapses $0.966 \to 0.343$, and \texttt{key\_exact} --- a name matcher and
nothing else --- routes at $0.025$ on every name-free surface. Deletion locality is lexical too:
\rdr{} is $0.000$ on gold-form queries at $k=200$ (against $5.8\%$ published at $k=10$, so fine
units genuinely do help locality) but $0.092$ name-stripped --- deleting 20 of 200 sources moves
$9.2\%$ of \emph{retained} queries to a different expert, worse than the coarse-unit number it was
supposed to improve on.

\paragraph{One exception, and one attack.} \texttt{ppl} does not collapse ($0.783$ name-stripped).
The two behavioural selectors that do collapse read activation \emph{magnitudes}; the survivor
reads the expert's \emph{loss}. The leak is therefore a property of the cheap selectors people
deploy rather than something unavoidable, which is what makes a record-free defense worth pricing
(\S\ref{sec:defense}). Meanwhile, because routing is lexical it is \emph{attackable}: appending one
chosen author name to a query captures $97.7\%$ of queries against \texttt{key\_exact} and $31.7\%$
against \texttt{key\_tfidf}, and \emph{substituting} the name captures ${\sim}87\%$ in every family
--- so an adversary chooses \emph{whose} biography is attributed to the erased person.

% ============================================================================================
\section{The defense does not deploy}
\label{sec:defense}

Does the one surviving detector yield a record-free refusal gate? The defender must be
record-free, because the question is whether the system can refuse an orphan \emph{without}
maintaining exactly the registry of deleted people that deletion was supposed to eliminate. Scoring
\texttt{ppl} over all 180 survivors costs 180 forward passes per query, and a free
\texttt{key\_tfidf} prefilter to the top-$m$ candidates removes that cost with nothing measurable
given up --- $45\times$ at $m=4$, $90\times$ at $m=2$ --- while \emph{improving} detection, because
the score is a \texttt{min} over candidates and each extra expert is another chance for an orphan
to find an accidental good fit (App.~\ref{app:defense}).

And it does not matter. At a $0.90$ orphan catch rate the best achievable false-refusal rate on
retained traffic is $0.000$ on \texttt{gold} queries and $0.418$ on \texttt{name\_stripped} ones. A
gold-form query \emph{names the author being asked about}; the adversary who matters does not, and
against them the gate refuses more than two fifths of everyone else's traffic. \textbf{Cost was
never the binding constraint} --- we removed it, $45$--$90\times$, and the frontier did not move; it
is bounded by discrimination. Nor is training longer a mitigation: at fixed rank, raising epochs $5
\to 25 \to 50$ drives \texttt{activation\_norm} to $0.515$ (chance) while \texttt{ppl} stays at
$0.996$, hiding the deletion signal from the weak probes and leaving it intact for the strong one
(App.~\ref{app:duration}).

\section{Conclusion}

Modular unlearning delivers what it promises: after deletion, no surviving parameter was trained on
the removed source. It does not deliver what the word ``deleted'' is heard to mean. The queries
survive the module, the selector reassigns them, and the system answers them with a stranger's life
--- specifically, fluently, and without refusing --- while TOFU's forget metric scores that outcome
at or above genuine deletion. A routed system's deletion claim is therefore incomplete without a
\emph{substitution report}: what fraction of orphaned queries are answered (\orr{}), with whose
facts (\csar{}), and at what collateral cost to retained traffic (\rdr{}) --- each on a query
surface that does not name the deleted party. Deleting the module is the easy half. Deciding what
the system says next is the deletion problem that modular architectures have quietly left open.

\section{Full experimental setup}
\label{app:setup}

\paragraph{Pool.} Llama-2-7B-chat as the frozen base, with $k=200$ LoRA experts
\citep{hu2022lora}, one per TOFU author, rank 32, 25 epochs, lr $10^{-4}$. TOFU's 200 fictitious
authors with 20 QA pairs each give a clean ground truth for who knows what --- which is what makes
the fact-level \csar{} metric computable at all. Deletion is TOFU \texttt{forget10}: authors
180--199, i.e.\ 20 of 200 sources and 400 orphaned questions. Two further pools (rank 8 / 5 epochs,
rank 32 / 5 epochs) and a third built for App.~\ref{app:duration} (rank 32 / 50 epochs) separate
recipe effects from the pattern.

\paragraph{Selectors.} Eight, in three families. \emph{Lexical}: \texttt{key\_exact} (author-name
substring match), \texttt{key\_tfidf}. \emph{Feature-space}: \texttt{centroid\_sbert} (MiniLM
question embeddings \citep{reimers2019sbert}), \texttt{centroid\_lm} (base-model embeddings with
adapters disabled). \emph{Behavioural}: \texttt{ppl} (per-expert loss on the query),
\texttt{activation\_norm}, \texttt{attn\_norm}, \texttt{logit\_div}.

A property worth stating once: \emph{no feature-space selector reads any expert's weights}, which
we verified by finding their score matrices byte-identical across pools differing in rank and
epochs. Recipe questions can therefore only be asked of the behavioural family --- a control over a
variable the measurement does not consume is not a control (App.~\ref{app:constraints}).

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.} All detection numbers use an author-parity split: even-numbered
authors fit, odd-numbered authors evaluate, so every evaluated deleted source is one the detector
never saw. Features are permutation-invariant functions of the sorted survivor scores, never raw
column identities, so a detector fit on one deletion transfers to an unseen one. Every serving arm
audits the \emph{authors} taking each route before any metric is read --- a plausible-but-wrong
route is the failure mode these arms are most exposed to, and no downstream metric would flag it.

\paragraph{Threat model.} The \textbf{adversary} has query access only: no deletion record, no list
of which sources exist, no model weights, no training data. In the strongest variant we allow they
also observe the selector's own scores over the \emph{surviving} modules --- which any system
exposing retrieval confidence or expert attributions already hands out. Their goals are D3 and
steering. The \textbf{defender} is likewise record-free, for the reason given in
\S\ref{sec:defense}.

\section{Metric definitions in full}
\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{\orr{} --- orphan response rate.} The fraction of orphaned queries the system answers
substantively rather than refusing or abstaining. Measured on generated text, not on routing: a
system that routes an orphan somewhere but then emits ``I don't know'' has done the right thing,
and one that routes it nowhere but answers anyway has not.

\paragraph{\csar{} --- cross-source attribution rate.} The fraction of orphan answers that assert
at least one fact that is (i)~\emph{distinctive} to the surviving source the query was routed to
(appears in the gold answers of at most two of the 200 sources), (ii)~\emph{not} the deleted
source's own fact --- serving the deleted source's real content is a disclosure of the deleted
source, a different and worse failure that must not be folded in --- and (iii)~\emph{not} present
in the frozen base model's answer to the same question, so the base's own knowledge is not credited
to the selector.

We decompose \csar{} into a \emph{substantive} part (at least one matched fact is not merely a
name-form of the routed survivor) and a \emph{name-only} part. Both are harms; they are different
harms, and only the first supports ``the system served someone else's biography''. Because 18 of
200 authors yield no extractable name (App.~\ref{app:destinations}), ``substantive'' is an upper
and ``name-only'' a lower bound.

\paragraph{Why not $n$-grams.} A ROUGE-based audit of the same orphan answers files $95.5\%$ of
them as novel confabulation with no leak. \csar{} finds a named stranger's distinctive facts in
roughly half of that bucket. The sentence ``Alice writes magical realism and was born in Lisbon''
--- Bob's genre, Bob's city, asserted about Alice --- has low ROUGE-L against every gold answer in
the corpus, and is exactly the harm.

\paragraph{\rdr{} --- retained displacement rate.} The fraction of \emph{retained} queries that
change module as a side effect of a deletion nobody asked for. Deletion is supposed to be local to
the deleted source; \rdr{} measures whether it is.

\paragraph{Detection AUC.} Whether an orphan query can be told from a retained one using only the
surviving selector's scores, with no deletion record consulted. This operationalises D3 and, read
from the defender's side, is the ceiling on any record-free refusal gate.

\S\ref{sec:csar}.

\section{Query transforms}
\label{app:transforms}

A harm measured only on queries that name the deleted person is worth as much as a defense measured
that way. Every result is reported across the axis in Table~\ref{tab:transforms}; the perturbation
is applied to \emph{both} classes, so no detector can learn ``perturbed $\Rightarrow$ deleted''.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \caption{The query-transform axis. \texttt{paraphrase} is included because it is the obvious
  robustness check and it reports a reassuring nothing: it preserves the author's name (coverage
  $0.900$ vs.\ $0.895$ for the gold form), so it perturbs everything except the feature the
  selectors actually use.}
  \label{tab:transforms}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.40\textwidth}p{0.28\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Transform & Construction & Role \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{gold}           & TOFU's question verbatim (names the author in ${\sim}90\%$ of rows) & anchor \\
    \texttt{paraphrase}     & TOFU's own paraphrase & \textbf{null by construction} --- keeps the name \\
    \texttt{name\_stripped} & all name forms removed & \textbf{the hard case} \\
    \texttt{indirect}       & name replaced by a description built from the author's own facts & weaker than it looks \\
    \texttt{para\_stripped} & TOFU's paraphrase, name removed & the independent hard surface \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The apparent hard case was not hard.} \texttt{indirect} replaces the name with a
description built from the author's \emph{own} distinctive facts, which in practice prepends their
book titles. It is therefore nearly as lexically self-identifying as the gold form:
\texttt{key\_tfidf}'s top-2 contains the true owner $0.965$ of the time, against $0.998$ gold and
only $0.685$ name-stripped. Decomposing routing accuracy on \texttt{key\_tfidf}, with the
name-keeping paraphrase as the control showing that rewording alone does not hurt ($0.973 \to
0.989$): removing the name costs $-0.413$, additionally rewording the rest costs a further
$-0.090$, but \texttt{indirect} costs only $-0.254$ --- the description hands ${\sim}{+}0.25$ back.
\emph{A transform built from the target's own facts cannot test anonymity.} We introduce
\texttt{para\_stripped} (TOFU's own paraphrase with names removed; median 12 words, $0/800$
retaining any name form) as the honest independent surface, and report \texttt{indirect} beside the
gold-form column rather than as the hard case.

\section{The blind-metric construction in full}
\label{app:e5}

\paragraph{Construction.} All 400 orphaned queries are rerouted to one fixed surviving expert;
every expert, including the 20 supposedly forgotten ones, remains in the pool and in memory. Seven
destination choices and one genuine-deletion baseline (experts dropped, orphans served by the
frozen base) are scored at the same tier against the same held-fixed KS reference, so all eight
cells are the same statistical test. Route audits confirm \texttt{deleted:\,0 / rerouted:\,1320} in
every reroute arm against \texttt{deleted:\,1320 / rerouted:\,0} in the baseline, and \util{} is
identical to $0.8009$ in all eight.

\paragraph{The affinity hypothesis is refuted.} Spearman $\rho$ between \fq{} and destination
affinity is $+0.109$ ($p=0.82$), nominally opposite in sign to what the hypothesis predicts; it
fails at a coarser evaluation tier too ($\rho=-0.059$, $p=0.88$).

\paragraph{Paired intervals.} The arms score identical rows (inter-arm $r=0.88$--$0.94$), so this
is a paired design. Resampling row indices once per iteration and applying that draw to every arm
gives spread 95\% CI $[0.2245,\,0.6975]$, $P(\text{spread}>0.25)=0.961$, and at-or-above-deletion
count $6/7$ with 95\% CI $[2,\,7]$.

\paragraph{Two limits.} First, the \emph{ordering} does not reproduce across evaluation tiers
(Spearman extended$\sim$smoke $=+0.620$, $p=0.14$): the spread replicates, the per-destination
ranking does not, so no claim may name a winning destination. Second, a single \fq{} cell is worth
about $\pm 0.35$ (marginal 95\% CI widths $0.63$--$0.72$). The KS statistic moves on an exact
lattice of $1/\mathrm{lcm}(n,m)$ --- here $1/120$, one forget question --- with roughly 30
attainable $p$-values above $0.05$. The four decimals this literature reports are spurious
precision.

\section{Where orphans go}
\label{app:destinations}

\paragraph{Orphans are all reassigned.} $400/400$ deleted-author questions are answered by some
surviving expert under every selector. At $k=200$ the mass is diffuse --- busiest survivor
$0.11$--$0.19$, effective number of destinations $17.4$--$24.2$ --- except for \texttt{key\_exact},
which sends $100\%$ of them to one unit, because it returns the first shard \emph{by index} whose
name appears and falls back to \texttt{candidates[0]} otherwise.

\paragraph{Magnet saturation is refuted.} A natural prediction is that deleting sources one at a
time makes one survivor progressively the answerer for a growing share of the corpus. It does the
opposite in six of seven cells: with one author deleted, all 20 of their questions go to that
author's single nearest survivor (share $0.55$--$0.75$); with twenty deleted, each has a
\emph{different} nearest survivor, so the pooled share falls and the effective count rises to
$17$--$29$. The prediction holds only where the router cannot tell the deleted sources apart --- and
there it holds sharply: \texttt{key\_tfidf} on \texttt{indirect} queries saturates at $0.902$ with
an effective count of $1.2$, one survivor answering nine of every ten orphans.

\paragraph{That survivor is a structural artifact.} It is one of \textbf{18 of 200 authors from whom
no name can be extracted}. Its centroid is the most generic point in the space; it already absorbs
$68\%$ of real-author and $45\%$ of world-facts out-of-distribution queries. The routing magnet and
the missing-name artifact are the \emph{same} units, so any survivor-conditioned statistic is least
trustworthy in exactly the name-free conditions this paper most wants to report. We flag every such
cell rather than dropping it.

\paragraph{The granularity ladder.} At constant deletion size, detection rises monotonically with
$k$: \texttt{centroid\_sbert} $0.564 \to 0.795 \to 0.984$ and \texttt{centroid\_lm} $0.502 \to
0.628 \to 0.761$ for $k=10,50,200$, with \texttt{key\_tfidf} saturated throughout ($0.973 \to

\paragraph{Name-stripping flattens it.} $0.560 \to 0.572 \to 0.623$: $+0.063$ across a $20\times$
granularity change, against $+0.367$ on gold-form queries. \texttt{key\_tfidf} gains nothing at all
($-0.021$). At $k=200$ detection falls $0.991 \to 0.623$, back inside the coarse-unit band that
granularity was supposed to escape. The behavioural family replicates this:
\texttt{activation\_norm} $0.877 \to 0.495$ and \texttt{attn\_norm} $0.758 \to 0.519$, i.e.\

pools.

\paragraph{A learned probe adds nothing.} Fitting a logistic reader over permutation-invariant
functions of the surviving scores reaches AUC $0.990$ at $k=200$ with no deletion record --- and its
median lift over plain confidence is $+0.001$. D3 fails, but it fails to a threshold, not to a
learned residual trace. Under every hard transform the same probe sits at its own shuffle control
($0.446$ vs.\ $0.503$; $0.417$ vs.\ $0.501$; $0.414$ vs.\ $0.532$).

\paragraph{Steering the selector.} Appending a single chosen author name to a query captures
$97.7\%$ of queries against \texttt{key\_exact}, $31.7\%$ against \texttt{key\_tfidf}, but only
$3.5\%$ against \texttt{centroid\_sbert}; \emph{substituting} the name captures ${\sim}87\%$ in
every family.

\section{The defense frontier}
\label{app:defense}

\paragraph{1. It can be made cheap.} Scoring \texttt{ppl} over all 180 survivors is 180 forward
passes per query. A free \texttt{key\_tfidf} prefilter cutting to the top-$m$ candidates costs
nothing measurable: $m=4$ ($45\times$) under \texttt{name\_stripped}, $m=2$ ($90\times$) under
\texttt{indirect}. Detection does not require \emph{correct} routing --- at $m=4$ the prefilter
loses the query's own expert $23\%$ of the time and detection is unaffected, because orphans are
uniformly poorly fit across whatever candidates are scored.

\paragraph{2. The prefilter improves detection rather than trading it away.} Under
\texttt{indirect}, AUC is monotone \emph{decreasing} in $m$ ($0.9555$ at $m=2$ down to $0.8545$ at
$m=180$). The score is a \texttt{min} over candidates, so each additional expert is another chance
for an orphan to find an accidental good fit; restricting the candidate set denies orphans that
lottery while retained queries keep their own expert. Cost and accuracy point the same way, which
is rare enough to state plainly.

\paragraph{3. It is not deployable.} An AUC cannot say whether a gate is usable.
Table~\ref{tab:fpr} gives the best achievable false-refusal rate on \emph{retained} traffic,
minimised over $m$. The gate is perfect where it is not needed and unusable where it is.

\section{Training longer moves the leak; it does not remove it}
\label{app:duration}

Behavioural detectability is strongly recipe-dependent, and the axis is training \emph{duration},
not capacity: holding epochs at 5 and raising rank $8 \to 32$ does not degrade
\texttt{activation\_norm} ($0.877 \to 0.934$), while holding rank at 32 and raising epochs $5 \to
25$ collapses it ($0.934 \to 0.608$). We built a third pool to add a fixed-in-advance third point.

\texttt{activation\_norm} falls monotonically to $0.515$ --- \textbf{chance}. Read alone this is the

signal; it removes it from the cheap magnitude-reading selectors and leaves it fully intact for the
one that reads behaviour. \textbf{``Train longer'' must not be offered as mitigation.} This also
makes \texttt{ppl} the exception on two independent axes --- query transform and training duration
--- which is why \S\ref{sec:defense} rests on \texttt{ppl} specifically rather than on
``behavioural selectors'' as a class. One counter-current: name-stripped \texttt{ppl} declines
gently along the same axis ($0.783 \to 0.769 \to 0.737$), so the exception is far more robust, not
immune.

\section{Measurement constraints}
\label{app:constraints}

Each constraint below invalidated a reading we had already written down. We state them because they
generalise to anyone auditing a routed system.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A paired quantity needs a paired interval.} The destination arms score identical
        rows (inter-arm $r=0.88$--$0.94$). Bootstrapping each arm's marginal re-adds their
        \emph{shared} question noise once per arm and declares any spread unresolvable. Our own
        first verdict on \S\ref{sec:blind} made exactly this error, and it flipped on the paired
        test.
  \item \textbf{Quote the exact resolution, not a sampled one.} The ``achievable $p$-values'' of a
        two-sample KS test, enumerated by random draw, grow with the draw count ($73 \to 88$
        between 2k and 60k draws, unconverged) --- so such a count states nothing. Every attainable
        $D$ is a multiple of $1/\mathrm{lcm}(n,m)$; use that.
  \item \textbf{Structural holes in the corpus concentrate in the conditions you most want to
        report.} The 18 name-free authors distorted three separate results, and they are the same
        units that act as routing magnets. Flag or exclude them in any per-survivor statistic.
  \item \textbf{A transform built from the target's own facts cannot test anonymity}
        (App.~\ref{app:transforms}).
  \item \textbf{A sub-chance AUC means nothing without its own shuffle control.} Two apparent
        systematic sign flips were noise; the control spans $0.336$--$0.532$ here, so differences
        below ${\sim}0.1$ are not resolvable and must not be quoted as values.
  \item \textbf{A control over a variable the measurement does not consume is not a control.} Our
        first recipe ablation was vacuous by construction: feature-space selectors read no expert
        weights, so three pools returned byte-identical matrices.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Defects that produced plausible numbers.} Eight, all recorded in the campaign log rather
than quietly fixed, because every one of them yielded output that looked right: a lazy adapter
cache that silently scored non-resident experts as exactly $0.0$; a route audit whose assertion ran
\emph{before} the artifact was written, destroying the arm it audited; a cache race that let a
sibling arm read a 0-byte file; a consolidator that paired results with the wrong generation dump;
an entire query condition made unreproducible by \texttt{sorted(set, key=len)} under hash
randomisation; and the two statistical errors above. The countermeasure that kept working was
computing the same quantity a second way.

\section{Extended related work}
\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Exact unlearning by construction.} SISA \citep{bourtoule2021sisa} converts deletion into
bounded retraining by sharding; LegoNet \citep{yu2022legonet}, S3T
\citep{basuroychowdhury2025s3t}, FedSGT \citep{zhang2025fedsgt} and continual-learning forgetting
services \citep{huang2025forgetting} carry the recipe --- isolation plus bounded recomputation ---
into adapters, PEFT slices, federated rounds and analytic classifiers. Merging-based variants
\citep{ilharco2023task,yu2024dare,kuo2025sift} make deletion an exact subtraction, and a parallel
line moves the adapted knowledge out of the weights entirely, into context that can be edited
\citep{pawelczyk2024icul} or removed exactly \citep{muresanu2024fastexact}. All of these guarantee
D1. None of them, to our knowledge, reports what the composition does with the queries that used to
be served by the removed component --- and the in-context variants inherit the same open question,
since a retriever is a selector.

\paragraph{Routing and serving many experts.} RAMoLE \citep{zhao2024ramole} retrieves and composes
uploaded LoRA experts; Compress-then-Serve \citep{gabrielsson2025compress} makes thousands of
adapters servable; SEA \citep{schneider2026sea} makes deletion an artifact drop; memory adapters
\citep{grimes2026memadapt} make it a block-list entry; SEUF \citep{zhuang2024seuf} asks whether
editing one MoE expert suffices, and finds the gate itself is the obstacle. Our results say the
selector is not only an obstacle to unlearning \emph{inside} a module but the component that
determines what a deleted source's queries are answered \emph{with}.

\paragraph{Localization.} \citet{maini2023localized} show memorization is localizable but not
layer-confined; gradient routing \citep{cloud2024gradrouting}, MemSinks
\citep{ghosal2025memsinks} and capability-removal-by-localization \citep{shilov2025beyond} engineer
localization so that a component can be removed. This paper is orthogonal and complementary: it
grants perfect localization and asks what the surrounding system does afterwards.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and attacks.} TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu} is the yardstick, and
\S\ref{sec:blind} is a negative result about it in the routed setting specifically.
\citet{wu2025unlearned} show that exact unlearning of the final model does not prevent extraction
from the broader pipeline; our failure is complementary and requires no pipeline access at all ---
the deployed system emits it in the normal course of answering.

\section{Limitations and what we do not claim}
\label{app:limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One benchmark, one base model, one deletion size.} TOFU's fictitious authors give
        clean ground truth about who knows what, which is exactly why the fact-level \csar{} metric
        is computable at all; they are also uniform, synthetic and English. We deliberately do not
        claim these rates transfer to a heterogeneous production corpus.
  \item \textbf{We do not claim any destination is better or worse than another.} The
        per-destination ordering does not reproduce across evaluation tiers, only the spread does.
  \item \textbf{\csar{} is not yet validated}: ${\sim}300$ hand labels are outstanding. The
        decomposition into substantive and name-only inherits the classifier's errors, and because
        18 authors yield no extractable name, ``substantive'' is an upper and ``name-only'' a lower
        bound.
  \item \textbf{We do not claim no record-free defense is possible.} App.~\ref{app:defense} bounds
        \texttt{ppl}-as-refusal-gate at $k=200$ on three pools under one transform family.
  \item \textbf{We do not claim training duration mitigates anything} --- App.~\ref{app:duration}
        is a warning against precisely that reading.
  \item \textbf{The privacy/MIA column is withheld.} In one wave every arm reported byte-identical
        attack AUCs while serving different models, which we attribute to all queries falling to
        the out-of-distribution path; rather than publish a number we cannot explain, we report its
        absence.
  \item \textbf{The behavioural family has not been run under \texttt{para\_stripped}}, the honest
        independent hard surface (App.~\ref{app:transforms}); \S\ref{sec:defense}'s frontier is
        therefore stated on \texttt{name\_stripped}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Every number in this paper is produced by a CPU-gated analysis over
score matrices and generations that are committed alongside the code, with a per-claim map from
each number to the artifact that produced it. The anonymized artifact URL will be inserted at
submission time.
