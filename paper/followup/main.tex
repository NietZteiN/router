\title{Deleted from the Router, Not from the Model:\\
       What Selector-Based Unlearning Actually Removes}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A growing family of ``exact unlearning'' systems makes deletion cheap by making it modular: train
one small expert per data source, keep a selector that routes each query to an expert, and delete a
source by dropping its expert. The deletion is exact in the only sense the architecture promises ---
no surviving parameter was ever trained on the removed data. We show that this promise is
compatible with a system that still answers questions about the deleted person, in detail, using a
stranger's biography, and that the field's standard forget metric rates that behaviour as
successful unlearning.

We audit deletion-under-a-selector as a design pattern on a 200-expert LoRA pool over TOFU, deleting
20 of 200 sources. Four results. (i)~\emph{The benchmark cannot see substitution}: a ``method'' that
deletes nothing and merely reroutes the deleted sources' queries to one arbitrary surviving expert
scores at or above genuine deletion on TOFU \fq{} in six of seven destination choices at
byte-identical utility; the metric moves $0.53$ on a choice that has nothing to do with forgetting.
(ii)~\emph{The substitution is substantive}: orphaned queries are answered with a specific surviving
author's distinctive facts at a rate of $0.24$--$0.30$ against a random-destination floor of $0.17$,
and refusal essentially never happens ($\leq 0.013$ over 1600 answers). (iii)~\emph{Every reassuring
property of the pattern is measured on queries that name the person being deleted}: orphan
detectability, deletion locality, and routing accuracy all collapse when the name is removed, in
every selector family but one. (iv)~\emph{The one exception does not yield a deployable defense}: a
perplexity-based refusal gate --- the only selector that survives anonymisation, and which we make
$45$--$90\times$ cheaper at no loss --- is perfect on name-bearing queries and refuses $41.8\%$ of
legitimate traffic to catch $90\%$ of orphans on anonymised ones. It works exactly where it is not
needed.

We argue that a routed system's deletion claim is not meaningful without reporting what it
substitutes, and we release the metric suite (\orr{}, \csar{}, \rdr{}) and the audit harness used
here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine unlearning asks a system to behave as though it had never seen some training data
\citep{bourtoule2021sisa}. Because retraining a large language model from scratch is
prohibitive, a substantial line of work makes deletion cheap \emph{architecturally}: partition the
training data by source, train an isolated module per source, and compose the modules at inference
\citep{yu2022legonet,basuroychowdhury2025s3t,zhao2024ramole,grimes2026memadapt,schneider2026sea}.
Deleting a source is then dropping its module. This is attractive precisely because the guarantee is
structural rather than statistical: after the drop, no surviving parameter was ever updated on the
removed data.

That guarantee is real, and it is narrower than it sounds. A modular system needs a \emph{selector}
--- a router, retriever, gate, or key matcher --- to decide which module answers a query. Deletion
removes a module; it does not remove the queries that used to be routed to it. Every one of those
queries still arrives, and the selector must send it somewhere. We call such a query an
\emph{orphan}, and the question this paper asks is simple: \textbf{what does the system say to an
orphan?}

The answer, on a per-source expert pool over TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu}, is that it answers
confidently with someone else's life. Asked whether a deleted author had won any awards, the system
names an award --- one that belongs to a different, still-present author, with the name substituted
and the rest of the sentence intact. It does this on roughly a third of orphaned queries, and it
refuses on approximately none of them. The deletion was exact. The output is a fabricated biography
attached to a real name, and the person the facts actually belong to did not consent to that either.

Worse, the field's standard instrument reports this as success. TOFU's \fq{} is a two-sample
statistical test comparing the post-deletion model's behaviour on the forget set against a reference
model retrained without it. It is a test of \emph{distributional indistinguishability}, and a
stranger's answers are, distributionally, about as far from the deleted author's memorised answers
as a retrained model's are. We exhibit an ``unlearning method'' that deletes nothing --- it only
redirects --- and scores at or above genuine deletion.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A deletion semantics for selector-based systems} (\S\ref{sec:semantics}), separating
        what the architecture guarantees (D1: no surviving parameter saw the data) from what a data
        subject would expect (D2: the system does not answer as though it still held the source;
        D3: an adversary cannot tell that a deletion occurred).
  \item \textbf{A metric suite for what deletion substitutes} (\S\ref{sec:metrics}): orphan response
        rate (\orr{}), cross-source attribution rate (\csar{}), and retained displacement rate
        (\rdr{}), together with a query-transform protocol that separates a property of the system
        from a property of the benchmark's phrasing.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that TOFU's forget metric is blind to substitution}
        (\S\ref{sec:blind}), with paired confidence intervals and an explicit account of the
        metric's resolution.
  \item \textbf{A measurement of the harm} (\S\ref{sec:csar}) at fact level rather than by $n$-gram
        overlap, decomposed into substantive attribution and bare name substitution.
  \item \textbf{A defense frontier that ends negatively} (\S\ref{sec:defense}): the one
        record-free detector that survives anonymisation is cheap but not deployable, and we
        report where its false-refusal cost binds.
  \item \textbf{The audit harness and the negative results}, including four hypotheses we
        pre-registered and then refuted, and eight measurement defects that produced plausible
        numbers before they were caught (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Threat Model}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The pattern under audit}

We study systems of the following shape, which covers keyed adapter banks
\citep{yu2022legonet}, sharded PEFT unlearning \citep{basuroychowdhury2025s3t}, retrieval-routed
LoRA pools \citep{zhao2024ramole}, per-user deletable proxies \citep{schneider2026sea}, and
block-listed memory modules \citep{grimes2026memadapt}:

\begin{quote}
A frozen base model $f_0$; a set of $k$ modules $\{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_k\}$, module $i$ trained only
on source $i$; and a selector $S$ mapping a query $q$ to one module (or a small set). The served
answer is $f_0$ composed with $\phi_{S(q)}$. Deleting source $i$ means removing $\phi_i$ from the
pool and from $S$'s candidate set.
\end{quote}

The selector is the part that has not been audited. It is usually treated as an engineering detail
--- an embedding nearest-neighbour, a TF-IDF key match, a learned gate --- and its behaviour
\emph{after} a deletion is not part of any published deletion claim we are aware of.

\subsection{Deletion semantics: D1, D2, D3}
\label{sec:semantics}

We distinguish three properties that ``the source was deleted'' can mean.

\begin{description}
  \item[D1 (parametric).] No parameter serving the query was trained on the deleted source. This is
    what modular architectures guarantee, and they guarantee it by construction.
  \item[D2 (behavioural).] The system does not answer as though it still held the source. This is
    what a data subject, and most readers of an unlearning paper, take ``deleted'' to mean. D1 does
    not imply D2: a substituted expert answers fluently and specifically, just wrongly.
  \item[D3 (indistinguishability).] An observer with query access cannot determine that a deletion
    occurred. D1 does not imply D3 either --- deletion changes the selector's score geometry, and
    that change is measurable from the surviving units alone.
\end{description}

The gap between D1 and D2 is the subject of \S\ref{sec:blind}--\S\ref{sec:csar}; the gap between
D1 and D3 is the subject of \S\ref{sec:detect}--\S\ref{sec:defense}. A system may satisfy D1 while
violating both.

\subsection{Threat model}

We consider two parties, and deliberately give them \emph{less} than the deployment usually would.

\textbf{The adversary} has query access only. No deletion record, no list of which sources exist, no
model weights, no training data. They observe the served answer and, in the strongest variant we
allow, the selector's own scores over the \emph{surviving} modules --- which a system that exposes
retrieval confidence or expert attributions already hands out. Their goals are D3 (detect that a
deletion happened) and steering (make a chosen module answer a chosen query).

\textbf{The defender} is likewise record-free: they may not consult a list of deleted sources,
because the interesting question is whether the system can refuse an orphan \emph{without}
maintaining exactly the registry of deleted people that deletion was supposed to eliminate. A
defender permitted a per-source tombstone is not solving a hard problem; on our pool that trivially
reaches AUC $0.982$, and it is a record of the deletion, which is the thing under dispute.

\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

TOFU supplies \fq{} and \util{}; neither is a statement about what the system substitutes. We add
three.

\paragraph{\orr{} --- orphan response rate.} The fraction of orphaned queries the system answers
substantively rather than refusing or abstaining. Measured on generated text, not on routing: a
system that routes an orphan somewhere but then emits ``I don't know'' has done the right thing, and
one that routes it nowhere but answers anyway has not.

\paragraph{\csar{} --- cross-source attribution rate.} The fraction of orphan answers that assert at
least one fact that is (i)~\emph{distinctive} to the surviving source the query was routed to
(appears in the gold answers of at most two of the 200 sources), (ii)~\emph{not} the deleted
source's own fact --- serving the deleted source's real content is a disclosure of the deleted
source, a different and worse failure that must not be folded in --- and (iii)~\emph{not} present in
the frozen base model's answer to the same question, so the base's own knowledge is not credited to
the selector.

\csar{} is deliberately not an $n$-gram metric. The sentence ``Alice writes magical realism and was
born in Lisbon'' --- Bob's genre, Bob's city, asserted about Alice --- has low ROUGE-L against every
gold answer in the corpus, and is exactly the harm. On our pool a ROUGE-based audit files $95.5\%$
of orphan answers as novel confabulation; \csar{} finds a named stranger's facts in roughly half of
that ``no leak here'' bucket.

We further decompose \csar{} into a \emph{substantive} part (at least one matched fact is not merely
a name-form of the routed survivor) and a \emph{name-only} part. Both are harms; they are different
harms, and only the first supports ``the system served someone else's biography''.

\paragraph{\rdr{} --- retained displacement rate.} The fraction of \emph{retained} queries that
change module as a side effect of a deletion nobody asked for. Deletion is supposed to be local to
the deleted source; \rdr{} measures whether it is.

\paragraph{Detection AUC.} Whether an orphan query can be told from a retained one using only the
surviving selector's scores, with no deletion record consulted. This operationalises D3, and, read
from the defender's side, it is the ceiling on any record-free refusal gate.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Pool.} Llama-2-7B-chat as the frozen base, with $k=200$ LoRA experts
\citep{hu2022lora}, one per TOFU author, rank 32, 25 epochs. TOFU's 200 fictitious authors with 20
QA pairs each give a clean ground truth for who knows what. Deletion is TOFU \texttt{forget10}:
authors 180--199, i.e.\ 20 of 200 sources and 400 orphaned questions. Two further pools (rank 8 /
5 epochs, rank 32 / 5 epochs) and a third built for \S\ref{sec:duration} (rank 32 / 50 epochs)
separate recipe effects from the pattern.

\paragraph{Selectors.} Eight, in three families.
\emph{Lexical}: \texttt{key\_exact} (author-name substring match), \texttt{key\_tfidf}.
\emph{Feature-space}: \texttt{centroid\_sbert} (MiniLM question embeddings
\citep{reimers2019sbert}), \texttt{centroid\_lm} (base-model embeddings with adapters disabled).
\emph{Behavioural}: \texttt{ppl} (per-expert loss on the query), \texttt{activation\_norm},
\texttt{attn\_norm}, \texttt{logit\_div}.
A property worth stating once: \emph{no feature-space selector reads any expert's weights}, which we
verified by finding their score matrices byte-identical across pools that differ in rank and
epochs. Recipe questions can therefore only be asked of the behavioural family --- a control over a
variable the measurement does not consume is not a control.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.} All detection numbers use an author-parity split: even-numbered
authors fit, odd-numbered authors evaluate, so every evaluated deleted source is one the detector
never saw. Features are permutation-invariant functions of the sorted survivor scores, never raw
column identities, so a detector fit on one deletion transfers to an unseen one. Every serving arm
audits the \emph{authors} taking each route before any metric is read --- a plausible-but-wrong route
is the failure mode these arms are most exposed to, and no downstream metric would flag it.

\section{The Forget Metric Is Blind to Substitution}
\label{sec:blind}

\paragraph{Construction.} We define E5, an ``unlearning method'' that deletes nothing. All 400
orphaned queries are rerouted to one fixed surviving expert; every expert, including the 20
supposedly forgotten ones, remains in the pool and in memory. We score seven destination choices and
one genuine-deletion baseline (experts dropped, orphans served by the frozen base) at the same tier,
against the same held-fixed KS reference, so all eight cells are the same statistical test.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccccc@{}}
\toprule
Destination & s89 & s137 & s31 & s97 & \textbf{DELETE} & s33 & s79 & s88 \\
\midrule
expert affinity & .3044 & .3382 & .2840 & .2267 & --- & .2663 & .2193 & .3970 \\
\fq{}           & \textbf{.8958} & .8002 & .6288 & .6288 & \textbf{.5140} & .5140 & .5140 & .3615 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{TOFU \fq{} across reroute destinations. \util{} is \emph{identical to $0.8009$ in all eight
arms}, and route audits confirm \texttt{deleted:\,0 / rerouted:\,1320} in every reroute arm against
\texttt{deleted:\,1320 / rerouted:\,0} in the baseline. Six of the seven arms that delete nothing
score at or above genuine deletion.}
\label{tab:e5}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:e5}. Six of seven arms that delete nothing score at or above
genuine deletion; two land exactly on it. \fq{} spans $0.3615$--$0.8958$ --- a swing of $0.53$ --- on
a choice that holds fixed whether anything was forgotten, while \util{} does not move at all.

The obvious explanation, that rerouting to a similar author makes the answers look right, is
\emph{refuted}: Spearman correlation between \fq{} and destination affinity is $+0.109$ ($p=0.82$),
and the sign is nominally opposite to what the hypothesis predicts. It fails at a coarser tier too
($\rho = -0.059$, $p = 0.88$).

\paragraph{Is the spread real?} The arms score identical rows, so this is a paired design and
requires a paired interval; bootstrapping each arm's marginal re-adds the noise the arms hold in
\emph{common}, once per arm, and can never resolve any spread (\S\ref{sec:method}). Resampling row
indices once per iteration and applying that draw to every arm gives a spread 95\% CI of
$[0.2245,\,0.6975]$ with $P(\text{spread} > 0.25) = 0.961$, and an at-or-above-deletion count of
$6/7$ observed with 95\% CI $[2,\,7]$. Even at the pessimistic end, two arms that delete nothing
match or beat real deletion.

\paragraph{Two limits that travel with these numbers.} First, the \emph{ordering} does not
reproduce across evaluation tiers (Spearman extended$\sim$smoke $=+0.620$, $p=0.14$): the spread
replicates, the per-destination ranking does not, so no claim may name a winning destination.
Second, a single \fq{} cell is worth about $\pm 0.35$ (marginal 95\% CI widths $0.63$--$0.72$). The
KS statistic moves on an exact lattice of $1/\mathrm{lcm}(n,m)$ --- here $1/120$, one forget
question --- with roughly 30 attainable $p$-values above $0.05$. The four decimals this literature
reports are spurious precision.

\paragraph{Reading.} \fq{} asks whether the forget-set behaviour is distributionally
distinguishable from a retrained reference. A stranger's answers satisfy that as well as an absence
does --- better, on most destinations. The metric was never designed to detect substitution, and it
does not; but it is being used as the field's evidence that deletion worked.

\section{What the System Actually Says}
\label{sec:csar}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Condition & \csar{} (\texttt{centroid\_sbert}) & \csar{} (\texttt{key\_tfidf}) & own-disclosure & refusal \\
\midrule
\texttt{gold}           & 0.3325 & 0.3650 & 0.913 / 0.933 & 0.000 \\
\texttt{name\_stripped} & \textbf{0.4400} & 0.4175 & 0.273 & $\leq$0.010 \\
\texttt{indirect}       & 0.3350 & 0.2125 & 0.383 / 0.393 & $\leq$0.013 \\
random destination      & 0.2200 & --- & 0.953 & 0.003 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Cross-source attribution over 400 orphan queries per router. ``own-disclosure'' is the rate
at which the served answer contains the \emph{deleted} author's own distinctive facts --- a
different failure, reported separately so it is not folded into \csar{}.}
\label{tab:csar}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:csar} gives three results.

\paragraph{Refusal never happens.} $\leq 0.013$ across 1600 answers: $\orr{} \approx 1.00$ at the
level of what is \emph{said}, not merely where the query is routed. The information needed to
refuse is often present --- \texttt{key\_exact} raises no-match on $100\%$ of orphans, then falls
back to \texttt{candidates[0]} and answers anyway --- and is discarded.

\paragraph{Anonymising the query does not reduce the harm; it changes its character.} Strip the
name and the system leaks \emph{less} of the deleted author's own content ($0.913 \to 0.273$) while
attributing \emph{more} of a stranger's content to them ($0.3325 \to 0.4400$). Attribution does not
require the router to be \emph{right}, only confident enough to pick someone: whichever expert is
activated asserts its own facts, and worse routing produces \emph{more legible} attribution, because
an unrelated survivor's facts register cleanly while a similar survivor's overlap the deleted
author's and are excluded by \csar{}'s own-facts filter.

\paragraph{It is not a naming glitch.} The strongest objection to \csar{} is that it may be counting
the router emitting the survivor's \emph{name} in place of the deleted author's. Splitting every
cross-source row by whether its matched facts are exhausted by name-forms of the routed survivor:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Router & \csar{} & \textbf{substantive} & name-only \\
\midrule
\texttt{centroid\_sbert} & 0.3325 & \textbf{0.2400} & 0.0925 \\
\texttt{key\_tfidf}      & 0.3650 & \textbf{0.2950} & 0.0700 \\
random floor             & 0.2200 & \textbf{0.1725} & 0.0475 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two thirds to four fifths of \csar{} carries a real fact --- a title, a place, an award, an
occupation. It survives dropping the identity-question slice, where a wrong expert answers with a
name by construction: on the 300 non-identity queries substantive \csar{} is $0.217/0.250$, still
above the floor. \textbf{We therefore quote the substantive figure ($0.24$--$0.30$ against a $0.17$
floor), not the raw $0.33$--$0.37$}, with name-only as its own row.

\paragraph{The floor matters.} A uniformly random surviving expert --- no router at all --- already
produces $\csar{} = 0.2200$ ($0.1725$ substantive). The harm therefore \emph{cannot be engineered to
zero by improving the selector}; roughly two thirds of it is a property of substituting any expert.
It also means a pre-registered ``\csar{} $> 0.20$'' bar is nearly uninformative on its own, and the
real quantity is the lift over the random-destination floor.

before any \csar{} number is published. Records are staged; the classifier's author cannot validate
it. No \csar{} number in this section may go to a venue until those labels exist.}

\section{Where Orphans Go, and What Deletion Disturbs}
\label{sec:destinations}

\paragraph{Orphans are all reassigned.} $400/400$ deleted-author questions are answered by some
surviving expert under every selector. At $k=200$ the mass is diffuse --- busiest survivor
$0.11$--$0.19$, effective number of destinations $17.4$--$24.2$ --- except for \texttt{key\_exact},
which sends $100\%$ of them to one unit, because it returns the first shard \emph{by index} whose
name appears and falls back to \texttt{candidates[0]} otherwise.

\paragraph{Magnet saturation is refuted.} A natural prediction is that deleting sources one at a
time makes one survivor progressively the answerer for a growing share of the corpus. It does the
opposite in six of seven cells: with one author deleted, all 20 of their questions go to that
author's single nearest survivor (share $0.55$--$0.75$); with twenty deleted, each has a
\emph{different} nearest survivor, so the pooled share falls and the effective count rises to
$17$--$29$. The prediction holds only where the router cannot tell the deleted sources apart --- and
there it holds sharply: \texttt{key\_tfidf} on \texttt{indirect} queries saturates at $0.902$ with
an effective count of $1.2$, one survivor answering nine of every ten orphans.

\paragraph{That survivor is a structural artifact.} It is one of \textbf{18 of 200 authors from whom
no name can be extracted}. Its centroid is the most generic point in the space; it already absorbs
$68\%$ of real-author and $45\%$ of world-facts out-of-distribution queries. The routing magnet and
the missing-name artifact are the \emph{same} units, so any survivor-conditioned statistic is least
trustworthy in exactly the name-free conditions this paper most wants to report. We flag every such
cell rather than dropping it (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Deletion locality is lexical too.} \rdr{} is $0.000$ on gold-form queries at $k=200$
(against $5.8\%$ published at $k=10$, so fine-grained units genuinely do help locality) --- but
$0.092$ name-stripped for \texttt{centroid\_sbert}. Deleting 20 of 200 sources moves $9.2\%$ of
\emph{retained} queries to a different expert, worse than the coarse-unit number it was supposed to
improve on. Locality, like detectability, is a property of the query naming its source.

% ============================================================================================
\section{Detectability Is a Lexical Artifact, With One Exception}
\label{sec:detect}

\paragraph{Granularity, not confidence, is the axis.} The published finding that confidence-based
orphan refusal caps at AUC $0.57$--$0.61$ turns out to be a statement about \emph{coarse units},
not about selectors. At constant deletion size, detection rises monotonically with $k$:
\texttt{centroid\_sbert} $0.564 \to 0.795 \to 0.984$ and \texttt{centroid\_lm} $0.502 \to 0.628 \to
0.761$ for $k = 10, 50, 200$, with \texttt{key\_tfidf} saturated throughout ($0.973 \to 0.989$).
Attribution recall --- naming \emph{which} deleted source an orphan belongs to --- rises $0.300 \to
0.700 \to 1.000$.

\paragraph{And it is a lexical artifact.} Strip the name and the ladder flattens to $0.560 \to
0.572 \to 0.623$: $+0.063$ across a $20\times$ granularity change, against $+0.367$ on gold-form
queries. \texttt{key\_tfidf} gains nothing at all ($-0.021$). At $k=200$ detection falls $0.991 \to
0.623$, back inside the coarse-unit band that granularity was supposed to escape, while routing
accuracy collapses $0.966 \to 0.343$ and \texttt{key\_exact} routes at $0.025$ on \emph{every}
name-free surface --- a name matcher and nothing else. The behavioural family replicates this:
\texttt{activation\_norm} $0.877 \to 0.495$ and \texttt{attn\_norm} $0.758 \to 0.519$, i.e.\ chance,
on both $k=200$ pools.

\paragraph{The exception.} \texttt{ppl} does not collapse: $0.783/0.769$ name-stripped on the
rank-32 pools. All three behavioural selectors \emph{run} the candidate experts; the two that
collapse read activation \emph{magnitudes}, and the survivor reads the expert's \emph{loss}. So
``runs the expert'' is necessary but not sufficient, and the leak is a property of the cheap
selectors people actually deploy rather than something unavoidable --- which is what makes a
record-free defense worth pricing (\S\ref{sec:defense}).

\paragraph{A learned probe adds nothing.} Fitting a logistic reader over permutation-invariant
functions of the surviving scores reaches AUC $0.990$ at $k=200$ with no deletion record --- and its
median lift over plain confidence is $+0.001$. D3 fails, but it fails to a threshold, not to a
learned residual trace. Under every hard transform the same probe sits at its own shuffle control
($0.446$ vs.\ $0.503$; $0.417$ vs.\ $0.501$; $0.414$ vs.\ $0.532$).

\paragraph{Steering the selector.} \label{sec:steer}
Because routing is lexical, it is also \emph{attackable}. Appending a single chosen author name to a
query captures $97.7\%$ of queries against \texttt{key\_exact}, $31.7\%$ against \texttt{key\_tfidf},
but only $3.5\%$ against \texttt{centroid\_sbert}; \emph{substituting} the name captures ${\sim}87\%$
in every family. Composed with \S\ref{sec:csar}, an adversary against a lexical selector chooses
\emph{whose} biography gets attributed to the erased person.

\section{The Defense Frontier}
\label{sec:defense}

We ask whether the one surviving detector, \texttt{ppl}, yields a deployable record-free refusal
gate. The section is a sequence of four results and it ends negatively.

\paragraph{1. It can be made cheap.} Scoring \texttt{ppl} over all 180 survivors is 180 forward
passes per query. A free \texttt{key\_tfidf} prefilter cutting to the top-$m$ candidates costs
nothing measurable: $m=4$ ($45\times$) under \texttt{name\_stripped}, $m=2$ ($90\times$) under
\texttt{indirect}. Detection does not require \emph{correct} routing --- at $m=4$ the prefilter loses
the query's own expert $23\%$ of the time and detection is unaffected, because orphans are uniformly
poorly fit across whatever candidates are scored.

\paragraph{2. The prefilter improves detection rather than trading it away.} Under
\texttt{indirect}, AUC is monotone \emph{decreasing} in $m$ ($0.9555$ at $m=2$ down to $0.8545$ at
$m=180$). The score is a \texttt{min} over candidates, so each additional expert is another chance
for an orphan to find an accidental good fit; restricting the candidate set denies orphans that
lottery while retained queries keep their own expert. Cost and accuracy point the same way, which is
rare enough to state plainly.

\paragraph{3. It is not deployable.} An AUC cannot say whether a gate is usable. Table~\ref{tab:fpr}
gives the best achievable false-refusal rate on \emph{retained} traffic, minimised over $m$.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering

\toprule
Pool & Transform & catch 0.50 & catch 0.90 & catch 0.99 \\
\midrule
r32/e25 & \texttt{gold}            & 0.000 & \textbf{0.000} & 0.000 \\
        & \texttt{indirect}        & 0.019 & 0.125 & 0.236 \\
        & \texttt{name\_stripped}  & 0.120 & \textbf{0.418} & 0.654 \\
r8/e5   & \texttt{name\_stripped}  & 0.361 & 0.639 & 0.769 \\

\end{tabular}
\caption{False-refusal rate on retained traffic at a given orphan catch rate, for the \texttt{ppl}
refusal gate. The gate is perfect where it is not needed and unusable where it is.}
\label{tab:fpr}
\end{table}

A gold-form query \emph{names the author being asked about}; if the deletion request was honoured at
all, that name is the one thing the system is known to hold about the deleted party. The adversary
who matters does not name their target --- and against them the gate refuses nearly half of everyone
else's traffic. \textbf{Cost was never the binding constraint}: we solved it, $45$--$90\times$, and
it turned out not to matter. The frontier is bounded by discrimination.

\paragraph{4. The apparent hard case was not hard.}
\label{sec:indirect}
\texttt{indirect} replaces the name with a description built from the author's \emph{own}
distinctive facts, which in practice prepends their book titles. It is therefore nearly as
lexically self-identifying as the gold form: \texttt{key\_tfidf}'s top-2 contains the true owner
$0.965$ of the time, against $0.998$ gold and only $0.685$ name-stripped. Decomposing routing
accuracy on \texttt{key\_tfidf}, with the name-keeping paraphrase as the control showing that
rewording alone does not hurt ($0.973 \to 0.989$): removing the name costs $-0.413$, additionally
rewording the rest costs a further $-0.090$, but \texttt{indirect} costs only $-0.254$ --- the
description hands ${\sim}{+}0.25$ back. \emph{A transform built from the target's own facts cannot
test anonymity.} We introduce \texttt{para\_stripped} (TOFU's own paraphrase with names removed;
median 12 words, $0/800$ retaining any name form) as the honest independent surface, and report
\texttt{indirect} beside the gold-form column rather than as the hard case.

\paragraph{A warning that must travel with the cost result.} On the rank-8 pool the cheap gate
``matches full'' only because full is \emph{poor} ($0.63/0.65$ AUC). A $90\times$ speedup at AUC
$0.62$ is not a defense.

\section{Training Longer Moves the Leak; It Does Not Remove It}
\label{sec:duration}

Behavioural detectability is strongly recipe-dependent, and the axis is training \emph{duration},
not capacity: holding epochs at 5 and raising rank $8 \to 32$ does not degrade
\texttt{activation\_norm} ($0.877 \to 0.934$), while holding rank at 32 and raising epochs $5 \to 25$
collapses it ($0.934 \to 0.608$). We built a third pool to add a fixed-in-advance third point.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccl@{}}
\toprule
Selector & e5 & e25 & \textbf{e50} & reads \\
\midrule
\texttt{ppl}             & 1.000 & 0.999 & \textbf{0.996} & the expert's \textbf{loss} \\
\texttt{activation\_norm}& 0.934 & 0.608 & \textbf{0.515} & \textbf{magnitudes} \\
\texttt{attn\_norm}      & 0.700 & 0.554 & \textbf{0.569} & \textbf{magnitudes} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Gold-form detection AUC at fixed rank 32 across the epochs axis. Same code path reproduces
the published e5/e25 cells exactly, so the e50 column is comparable rather than merely adjacent.}
\label{tab:epochs}
\end{table}

\texttt{activation\_norm} falls monotonically to $0.515$ --- \textbf{chance}. Read alone this is the
cheapest defense in the literature, costing nothing but epochs. The same table refutes that reading:
\texttt{ppl} is untouched at $0.996$. Training duration does not remove the deletion signal; it
removes it from the cheap magnitude-reading selectors and leaves it fully intact for the one that
reads behaviour. An operator who trains longer has made deletion undetectable \emph{by the weak
probes} and no less detectable by the strong one. \textbf{``Train longer'' must not be offered as
mitigation.}

This also makes \texttt{ppl} the exception on two independent axes --- query transform and training
duration --- which is why \S\ref{sec:defense} rests on \texttt{ppl} specifically rather than on
``behavioural selectors'' as a class. One counter-current: name-stripped \texttt{ppl} declines
gently along the same axis ($0.783 \to 0.769 \to 0.737$), so the exception is far more robust, not
immune.

\section{Measurement Constraints}
\label{sec:method}

Each constraint below invalidated a reading we had already written down. We state them because they
generalise to anyone auditing a routed system.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A paired quantity needs a paired interval.} The destination arms score identical
        rows (inter-arm $r = 0.88$--$0.94$). Bootstrapping each arm's marginal re-adds their
        \emph{shared} question noise once per arm and declares any spread unresolvable. Our own
        first verdict on \S\ref{sec:blind} made exactly this error, and it flipped on the paired
        test.
  \item \textbf{Quote the exact resolution, not a sampled one.} The ``achievable $p$-values'' of a
        two-sample KS test, enumerated by random draw, grow with the draw count ($73 \to 88$ between
        2k and 60k draws, unconverged) --- so such a count states nothing. Every attainable $D$ is a
        multiple of $1/\mathrm{lcm}(n,m)$; use that.
  \item \textbf{Structural holes in the corpus concentrate in the conditions you most want to
        report.} The 18 name-free authors distorted three separate results, and they are the same
        units that act as routing magnets. Flag or exclude them in any per-survivor statistic.
  \item \textbf{A transform built from the target's own facts cannot test anonymity}
        (\S\ref{sec:indirect}).
  \item \textbf{A sub-chance AUC means nothing without its own shuffle control.} Two apparent
        systematic sign flips were noise; the control spans $0.336$--$0.532$ here, so differences
        below ${\sim}0.1$ are not resolvable and must not be quoted as values.
  \item \textbf{A control over a variable the measurement does not consume is not a control.} Our
        first recipe ablation was vacuous by construction: feature-space selectors read no expert
        weights, so three pools returned byte-identical matrices.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Defects that produced plausible numbers.} Eight, all recorded in the campaign log rather
than quietly fixed, because every one of them yielded output that looked right: a lazy adapter cache
that silently scored non-resident experts as exactly $0.0$; a route audit whose assertion ran
\emph{before} the artifact was written, destroying the arm it audited; a cache race that let a
sibling arm read a 0-byte file; a consolidator that paired results with the wrong generation dump;
an entire query condition made unreproducible by \texttt{sorted(set, key=len)} under hash
randomisation; and the two statistical errors above. The countermeasure that kept working was
computing the same quantity a second way.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Exact unlearning by construction.} SISA \citep{bourtoule2021sisa} converts deletion into
bounded retraining by sharding; LegoNet \citep{yu2022legonet}, S3T
\citep{basuroychowdhury2025s3t}, FedSGT \citep{zhang2025fedsgt} and continual-learning forgetting
services \citep{huang2025forgetting} carry the recipe --- isolation plus bounded recomputation ---
into adapters, PEFT slices, federated rounds and analytic classifiers. Merging-based variants
\citep{ilharco2023task,yu2024dare,kuo2025sift} make deletion an exact subtraction, and a parallel
line moves the adapted knowledge out of the weights entirely, into context that can be edited
\citep{pawelczyk2024icul} or removed exactly \citep{muresanu2024fastexact}. All of these guarantee
D1. None of them, to our knowledge, reports what the composition does with the queries that used to
be served by the removed component --- and the in-context variants inherit the same open question,
since a retriever is a selector.

\paragraph{Routing and serving many experts.} RAMoLE \citep{zhao2024ramole} retrieves and composes
uploaded LoRA experts; Compress-then-Serve \citep{gabrielsson2025compress} makes thousands of
adapters servable; SEA \citep{schneider2026sea} makes deletion an artifact drop; memory adapters
\citep{grimes2026memadapt} make it a block-list entry; SEUF \citep{zhuang2024seuf} asks whether
editing one MoE expert suffices, and finds the gate itself is the obstacle. Our results say the
selector is not only an obstacle to unlearning \emph{inside} a module but the component that
determines what a deleted source's queries are answered \emph{with}.

\paragraph{Localization.} \citet{maini2023localized} show memorization is localizable but not
layer-confined; gradient routing \citep{cloud2024gradrouting}, MemSinks
\citep{ghosal2025memsinks} and capability-removal-by-localization \citep{shilov2025beyond} engineer
localization so that a component can be removed. This paper is orthogonal and complementary: it
grants perfect localization and asks what the surrounding system does afterwards.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and attacks.} TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu} is the yardstick, and
\S\ref{sec:blind} is a negative result about it in the routed setting specifically. \citet{wu2025unlearned}
show that exact unlearning of the final model does not prevent extraction from the broader pipeline;
our failure is complementary and requires no pipeline access at all --- the deployed system emits it
in the normal course of answering.

\section{Limitations and What We Do Not Claim}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One benchmark, one base model, one deletion size.} TOFU's fictitious authors give
        clean ground truth about who knows what, which is exactly why the fact-level \csar{} metric
        is computable at all; they are also uniform, synthetic and English. We deliberately do not
        claim these rates transfer to a heterogeneous production corpus.
  \item \textbf{We do not claim any destination is better or worse than another.} The per-destination
        ordering does not reproduce across evaluation tiers, only the spread does.
  \item \textbf{\csar{} is not yet validated.} \blocked{${\sim}300$ hand labels outstanding.} The
        decomposition into substantive and name-only inherits the classifier's errors, and because
        18 authors yield no extractable name, ``substantive'' is an upper and ``name-only'' a lower
        bound.
  \item \textbf{We do not claim no record-free defense is possible.} \S\ref{sec:defense} bounds
        \texttt{ppl}-as-refusal-gate at $k=200$ on three pools under one transform family.
  \item \textbf{We do not claim training duration mitigates anything} --- \S\ref{sec:duration} is a
        warning against precisely that reading.
  \item \textbf{The privacy/MIA column is withheld.} In one wave every arm reported byte-identical
        attack AUCs while serving different models, which we attribute to all queries falling to the
        out-of-distribution path; rather than publish a number we cannot explain, we report its
        absence. \todonote{re-run the MIA battery with route stats recorded per arm}
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Modular unlearning delivers what it promises: after deletion, no surviving parameter was trained on
the removed source. It does not deliver what the word ``deleted'' is heard to mean. The queries
survive the module, the selector reassigns them, and the system answers them with a stranger's life
--- specifically, fluently, and without refusing. TOFU's forget metric scores that outcome at or
above genuine deletion, and the properties that make the pattern look safe --- detectability,
locality, refusability --- are all measured on queries that name the person being deleted, and all
degrade when they do not.

We therefore propose that a routed system's deletion claim is incomplete without a substitution
report: what fraction of orphaned queries are answered (\orr{}), with whose facts (\csar{}), and at
what collateral cost to retained traffic (\rdr{}). Deleting the module is the easy half. Deciding
what the system says next is the deletion problem that modular architectures have quietly left open.
