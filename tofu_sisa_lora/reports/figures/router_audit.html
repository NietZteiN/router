Delete a module. Where do its questions go?

Structural unlearning deletes a person by dropping the module trained on their data. But the questions that module used to answer don't vanish — they become orphans that have to route somewhere. This audit traces where, and finds that the choice of router — not the deletion — decides whether the deleted person can still be served.

Question 2 — can't we just detect low confidence and abstain?

No — an orphan's lookalike is as confident a match as a real query's own module.

Distribution of the router's top-1 similarity for orphaned questions (their module deleted) versus legitimate retain questions (module intact). If they separated, a threshold could tell them apart. They don't: the means sit 0.858 vs 0.877 and the curves sit almost on top of each other.

The lookalike a deleted person's question finds is just as good a match as the module a normal question legitimately owns. Confidence carries almost no information about whether the true module was deleted — which is exactly why the threshold fix below cannot work.
Question 3 — how badly does the threshold fix fail?

Every orphan you catch drags a legitimate query down with it.

Sweeping the abstain threshold τ trades off two rates: orphans correctly sent to the safe fallback (want high) against legitimate traffic wrongly abstained (want low). Because the distributions overlap, the two rise together — there is no τ that keeps one high and the other low.

retain traffic wrongly abstained
At the threshold that catches 90% of orphans, you also wrongly abstain 58% of real traffic. To keep the collateral under 5%, you catch only 15% of orphans. There is no acceptable operating point — the leak is not threshold-fixable.

Method check — the encoder confound

The earlier audit measured the wrong encoder.

A fine-tuned retriever had silently replaced the off-the-shelf index. Re-run with the correct (base-pinned) encoder, the leak is worse-behaved than it looked: fewer mis-routes, but the lookalikes it does find are near-perfect matches.

Off-the-shelf encoder: 18.5% sibling capture at a 0.98 match ratio (vs the FT encoder's 31.5% at 0.77). The cleaner encoder makes the surviving lookalike an even better stand-in for the deleted module.
Control — is the deletion itself real?

Yes: the deleted content stops answering; everything else is untouched.

With the router held fixed, the deleted authors' answer quality collapses (perplexity ↑) while retain authors are numerically unchanged. The leak is a routing effect, not a failure of the deletion.

Deleted-author perplexity 2.97 → 11.82 (forgotten); retain perplexity 2.88 → 2.85 (untouched). The module drop works; the question is only who the router reaches for next.
The design law
Structural deletion removes a person's module cleanly — but the questions it answered become orphans, and a similarity router re-serves the deleted person from a near-perfect lookalike that no confidence threshold can screen out. Only a hard identity router, which knows the person was deleted, is leak-safe.

This is why the same deleted model can look perfectly clean or quietly leaky depending only on how you serve it — and it is the routing half of the larger finding that a deleted owner is gone while a shared fact lives on in the surviving modules, waiting for a fuzzy router to reach it.
